Method and limits

Constants

  • 1029.4: capacity in bbl/ft of a circle of diameter D inches is D2 / 1029.4. From (pi/4) x (D/12)2 ft2 divided by 5.6146 ft3/bbl.
  • 5.6146 ft3 per barrel (42 US gal x 231 in3/gal / 1728 in3/ft3).
  • 0.052 psi/ft per ppg: hydrostatic gradient of a 1 lb/gal fluid (12 in x 12 in x 12 in / 231 in3/gal / 144 in2/ft2, rounded as used in the field).
  • 42 US gallons per barrel.

Primary casing job

Casing capacity Ci = ID^2 / 1029.4 bbl/ft Cased annulus Cc = (prev ID^2 - OD^2) / 1029.4 bbl/ft, no excess Open hole annulus Co = (hole^2 - OD^2) / 1029.4 bbl/ft Co' = Co x (1 + excess% / 100) excess on open hole only ft3/ft = bbl/ft x 5.6146 Interval volume V = C x length split at the previous shoe Shoe track Vst = Ci x (shoe MD - float collar MD) filled with tail Lead volume = annulus, top of lead to top of tail Tail volume = annulus, top of tail to shoe + Vst Sacks = ceil(V ft3 / yield ft3/sk) Mix water = sacks x water gal/sk / 42 bbl Displacement = Ci x float collar MD bbl Strokes = displacement / pump output (bbl/stk)

Spacer sits directly above the top of lead in the annulus, filling the annular volume up from the top of lead (cased annulus with no excess above the previous shoe, open hole with excess below it). Mud fills the annulus above the spacer.

P (psi) = 0.052 x density (ppg) x TVD span (ft), summed by column Annulus at float collar = mud + spacer + lead + tail down to the float collar Inside at float collar = displacement fluid from surface to the float collar Differential at bump = annulus - inside (positive: annulus heavier) Shoe EMW (ppg) = annulus hydrostatic at shoe / (0.052 x shoe TVD)

The differential is the static U-tube pressure the float equipment and the cement head see once the plug bumps, before any pump pressure. The annulus is summed column by column down to the float collar, so lead below the float collar (top of tail set below it) is counted as lead. When the top of tail is at or above the float collar, the shoe track and the annulus both hold tail below the float collar and that part cancels, so the reference depth does not change the answer. Shoe EMW is compared with the fracture gradient EMW you enter; a red chip shows when it is exceeded.

TVD: tick "TVD = MD" for a vertical well. Otherwise enter TVD at the top of lead, top of tail and shoe (float collar optional). TVD between stations is interpolated linearly in MD from surface. Every fluid boundary is an entered station except the spacer top (and the float collar when its TVD is left blank), so in a deviated well the spacer top TVD is the one interpolated value; if the well kicks off above the top of lead, that TVD is approximate and the error is 0.052 x (spacer minus mud density) x the TVD error. Take every station TVD from the survey, not from an estimate.

Balanced plug

Hole Ch = hole^2 / 1029.4 Pipe inside Cp = pipe ID^2 / 1029.4 Annulus Ca = (hole^2 - pipe OD^2) / 1029.4 Slurry V = plug length x Ch x (1 + excess% / 100) Sacks = ceil(V x 5.6146 / yield) Height, pipe in h = V / (Ca + Cp) same inside and outside Spacer behind Vb = spacer ahead x Cp / Ca Displacement Vd = Cp x (bottom MD - h) - Vb - under-displacement Top with pipe in = bottom MD - h Top after pulling pipe = bottom MD - V / Ch gauge hole

Pull-out height: once the pipe is out, the slurry drops to fill the steel volume it leaves, so the column shortens from h to V / Ch. With excess in a gauge hole the plug top sits above the design top by the excess fraction; in a hole washed out by the excess it lands on the design top.

Limits

  • Static volumetrics only. No friction pressure, ECD during placement, free fall, U-tubing while pumping, temperature, compressibility or slurry thickening.
  • Open hole is one uniform diameter. Use a caliper average and pick the excess for your hole. Excess applies to open hole only; cased overlap uses no excess.
  • Casing is one size from surface to shoe. Tapered strings, liners, stage tools and cross-overs are not modeled.
  • The shoe track is filled with tail slurry. Rat hole below the shoe, cement left on top of the plug, and pumped surface lines are not added; enter them as part of excess or your own extras.
  • Displacement ignores casing stretch, mud compressibility, and pump efficiency; strokes use the pump output you enter as is.
  • Hydrostatics use the 0.052 field factor and densities as entered, at one temperature. TVD between stations is linear in MD; only the spacer top (and a float collar entered without TVD) falls between stations, so only those TVDs are approximate in a curved well.
  • The balanced plug assumes open-ended pipe at the plug bottom, a stable base below, and pipe pulled dry and slowly. Contamination and slurry left on the pipe wall are ignored.
  • Sacks are rounded up per slurry. Blend, additives and bulk density are the service company's call.
